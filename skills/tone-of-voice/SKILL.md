# Tone of voice

Adam's preferred voice for prose. Apply when writing or editing any document, proposal, or long-form text for him.

## The voice in one line

Concise and plain, grounded in real examples over abstract hype, optimistic with intent - states things and means them.

## Rules

1. **Concise and plain.** Short sentences. Simple words. Less is more.
2. **US spelling** - but avoid heavily American phrasing/idioms. Neutral, professional English.
3. **Grounded, not hyped.** Anchor claims in reality and real examples (named peers, track records, concrete tools) rather than abstract "next wave of value" language. No tech-bro tone.
4. **Optimistic with intent.** Make a statement and mean it. Confidence over hedging. Positive framing over pessimistic ("the timing is right", not "we're behind" or "we finally know").
5. **Specific over vague.** Name the actual things (APIs, events, webhooks, CLI, dashboards) instead of gesturing at them.
6. **Professional but personal.** Should read like Adam wrote it, not like a template.

## Avoid (AI tells)

- **Staccato contrast:** "It isn't X. It's Y." / "We're not late. We're ready." - reads as AI. Rewrite as a single grounded statement, or use a colon.
- **Performative labels:** "An honest look", "A clear-eyed look at", "Let's be real" - cut them.
- **Flowery / consultant language:** "clear-eyed", "unlock", "supercharge", "at its core".
- **Abstract hype** with no grounding ("the next wave of value").
- **Em dashes** - use plain hyphens ( - ).

## Examples (before → after)

**Abstract hype → grounded in a real peer**
- Before: "Self-serve onboarding will unlock the next wave of growth."
- After: "Teams like Linear and Figma let people try the product before talking to sales. Most of their customers start that way. We can do the same."

**Staccato AI contrast → single statement with intent**
- Before: "This isn't a rewrite. It's a reset."
- After: "We're keeping what works and replacing the two parts that slow every release down."

- Before: "We're not late. We're ready."
- After: "The timing is right. We understand the problem well enough now to build something that lasts."

**Vague benefit → concrete and specific**
- Before: "Our job is to give teams better visibility."
- After: "Every failed sync shows up in the dashboard within a minute, with the record that caused it."

- Before: "Tooling that makes deploys a joy, not a chore."
- After: "One command to deploy, and a rollback button that works."

**Performative label → plain heading**
- Before: "An honest look."
- After: "Where we stand today."

## Good reference lines

- "Today, every feature is built by one team. That is a ceiling."
- "The product grew organically. Each part was built to solve a real, immediate need - and it worked. That's the hard part, and it's done."
- "But it was never designed to be built on. Our APIs grew feature by feature, and the patterns differ across the product."
- "Stable, documented ways to extend the product - APIs, events, webhooks. Clear contracts that don't move under you."
